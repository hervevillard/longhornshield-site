Home insurance

Homeowners, condo, renters and landlord policies — with the wind, hail and roof language read out loud before you sign a thing.

  • DwellingRebuilding the structure. The number that matters is what it would cost to rebuild in your area today, which has moved a long way from whatever was typed in when the policy was written.
  • Other structuresFences, detached garages, sheds, outbuildings. Usually set as a percentage of the dwelling limit rather than a figure anyone chose deliberately.
  • Personal propertyEverything inside, at either replacement cost or depreciated value. The gap between those two words shows up very sharply in a total loss.
  • Liability and medical paymentsInjuries and damage you are responsible for, at home or away, including most dog-related claims subject to breed rules.
  • Loss of useSomewhere to live while the house is repaired. Check the dollar cap, not just that the coverage exists.
  • Wind and hailOften carries its very own deductible, set as a percentage of the dwelling limit instead of a flat amount. On a $400,000 home, a 2% deductible is $8,000 out of your pocket before anything is paid.
  • FloodNever, ever included in a homeowners policy. Written separately through the NFIP or a private carrier, and there is normally a 30-day wait before it takes effect, so it is not something to arrange when a storm is already named.

Three things worth checking on the policy you already have

How your roof gets paid

Plenty of Texas policies now settle older roofs at depreciated value, or on a payment schedule by age. That one clause is the difference between a full replacement and a few thousand dollars.

Your wind and hail deductible

Find out whether it is a flat amount or a percentage, then do the arithmetic on the percentage. Almost nobody does this before their first claim.

Whether the rebuild figure is current

Construction costs rose faster than most renewal notices did. Being under-insured on dwelling can also cut what you collect on a partial loss, not just a total one.

Just send us the declarations page

It is the two-page summary at the front of your policy. With that one document we can tell you in a few minutes whether you are covered the way you think you are.
