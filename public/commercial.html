Commercial insurance

Contractors, shops, restaurants, trucking, offices and rental property. Forward us the certificate request and we will match the requirements.

  • General liabilityThird-party injury and property damage claims. Nine times out of ten this is the coverage a client, a landlord, or a general contractor is asking you to prove.
  • Commercial propertyBuildings, equipment, inventory and tenant improvements — plus business interruption, which pays you while a covered loss keeps the doors shut.
  • Business autoOwned, leased or hired vehicles. Personal auto policies exclude most business use, and people tend to discover that fact at the worst possible moment.
  • Workers' compensationEmployee injury coverage. Not mandatory for most private employers in Texas, and expected by very nearly every contract you will be asked to sign.
  • Professional and errors & omissionsClaims that come out of your advice or your professional work rather than anything physical.
  • Commercial umbrellaExtra limits sitting on top of your liability policies. Usually the cheapest way to satisfy a contract asking for a big number.
  • Business owner's policyGeneral liability and property bundled for smaller operations, normally cheaper than buying the pieces one at a time.

Certificates and additional insureds

If somebody has asked you for proof of insurance, the fastest route is to send us the request itself rather than describe it over the phone.

Same-day certificates

Send the name and address of whoever is asking and we will issue it, usually the same business day.

Contract requirements read for you

Forward the insurance section of the contract. We will tell you exactly which limits and endorsements you are short on.

Additional insured and waiver of subrogation

Two of the most commonly demanded endorsements and two of the most commonly missing. Both need to be on the policy, not merely typed onto a certificate.

Audits that do not ambush you

Payroll and revenue-rated policies get audited at the end of the term. We set the estimates honestly so the final bill is not a shock.

Tell us what the contract demands.

Describe the work you do and what you have been asked to carry. We will quote it and flag anything in the requirements that does not add up.
